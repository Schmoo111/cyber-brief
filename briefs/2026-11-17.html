1Top line

  • Supply-chain attack on Optimizely. Attackers abused this website-testing service to plant code on about 178 organisations' sites, including HSBC and Equifax, skimming payment cards and swapping crypto addresses.[1]
  • Visa confirms an incident at a supplier. Extortion group n0n claims data from Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase. Visa confirmed an incident at an unnamed Asia-Pacific provider and says payment data was not affected.[2][3]
  • Check Point firewall flaw exploited. CVE-2026-123039 gives attackers root control of gateways. CISA's federal deadline passed on 16 Nov.[5][6]
  • Extradition. US DOJ extradited an alleged member of the "8-Digits Team", an extortion crew linked to INC and Lynx ransomware.[8]

Bank relevance: today's main risk comes through third parties: website scripts, payment-network suppliers and edge devices such as firewalls.

CriticalConfidence: HighSupply chain

Optimizely Web Experimentation

What happened
Malicious code injected from 16 Nov, 04:21 UTC. Optimizely says an attacker exploited a flaw in its Experimentation signup flow; it has fixed the flaw and removed the code.[1]
Scope
Report URI found 188 infected scripts across 178 organisations, including HSBC, Equifax, Salesforce and CNN.
Payloads
Card skimmer, crypto address swapper, and redirects to iPhone/iPad exploit kits. Hudson Rock found no evidence devices were actually compromised. About $7,000 in crypto reached the attacker.
Actor
Unknown.
Who they are
Optimizely sells A/B testing and personalisation tools; its code runs directly on customers' web pages.
Why it matters
Any bank page loading Optimizely, Kissmetrics, StorifyMe or Minute-ly scripts could have exposed customer card details. Blocking the attacker's domain may not be enough: some poisoned scripts carried the full payload.
HighConfidence: MediumExtortion claim

Visa, Mastercard and JPMorgan Chase: n0n claim

Visa confirmed
An incident that began at an unnamed third-party provider in Asia-Pacific. Visa says its systems and card data were not affected.[2]
n0n claims
Data stolen from all three firms; a 48-hour deadline to negotiate before publishing.[2]
JPMorgan said
A concurrent outage was caused by a software bug, with no evidence of a cyberattack.[2]
Analysts say
Listings appear to involve marketing or partner data, not core payment data (Unverified).[3] Mastercard has made no statement found.
Who they are
Visa and Mastercard run global card payment networks. JPMorgan Chase is the largest US bank.
Why it matters
Possibly a supplier shared across the card ecosystem. Check whether your APAC payment and marketing vendors overlap with Visa's.

3Updates to open cases

Barracuda cloud breach. An attacker used a cloud access key Barracuda had given an AI vendor and never revoked, moving across about 10 cloud accounts over 44 hours (1–2 Nov). The activity matches ARTEX, an open-source AI-driven penetration-testing tool; Barracuda says that does not identify who ran it. Contacted customers must revoke and reissue XDR integration credentials.[7] Lesson: audit and revoke old keys held by suppliers.
