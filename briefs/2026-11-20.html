VMware vCenter: exploited for two months before a fix

Background: VMware (owned by Broadcom, a US chip and software company) makes vCenter, the central console that runs a data centre's virtual machines, software copies of servers. Whoever controls vCenter controls every server under it.

What happened
A flaw (rated 9.8 out of 10) lets an attacker with no password get an administrator session. Attackers created their own single sign-on (SSO) admin account and stole the keys used to sign login tokens, so they could forge logins. Broadcom patched it on 19 Nov.[1]
Actor
Mandiant (Google's incident-response unit) links it to Armory Castle, a suspected Chinese espionage group. They got in first through a recently patched flaw in BeyondTrust Remote Support (remote IT-help software), then used a custom tool, Bonesaw.
Status
Multiple incidents investigated. No fix for vCenter 7.0, which is out of support. Not yet on CISA's exploited list (KEV).
Action
Upgrade to the fixed 8.0 or 9.x releases, retire 7.0, and check for unknown SSO admin accounts.
Why it matters
Banks run their core workloads on vCenter; a forged-login attacker can stay hidden for months.
HighConfidence: MediumExploited vulnerability

Cisco Expressway: exploited after test code went public

Background: Cisco Expressway is a gateway that connects staff and outside callers to a company's video and phone system across the internet.

What happened
A flaw disclosed on 21 Oct lets an attacker run commands with full control ("root") on clustered Expressway servers without logging in. Russian security firm F6 says attackers broke into two internet-facing devices at a Russian organisation, spread to other servers and hunted for passwords. The attack code showed signs of AI-assistant use.[4]
Status
Single-source report; Cisco's advisory still said it knew of no attacks. Actor unknown.
Action
Upgrade to X15.5.20 or newer; there is no workaround.
HighConfidence: HighSupplier breach

N-able: email-security cloud breached

Background: N-able sells IT-management and security tools, mostly through IT service providers. Mail Assure and SpamExperts filter spam and malicious email for businesses.

Confirmed
Unknown attackers accessed a server in the hosted cloud; an outage from 16 Nov was re-classed as a security incident on 18 Nov. Mail still flows, but the management portal, APIs, quarantine and logs were offline in the Americas, Europe and Asia-Pacific. N-able says there is no evidence customer data or mailboxes were accessed.[3]
Why it matters
Without quarantine and logs, suspicious email is harder to investigate. Check whether you or your IT providers use these services.
MediumConfidence: HighGovernment

European Commission: attack used an AI hacking tool

Background: The Commission is the EU's executive arm. ARTEX is an open-source AI tool that automates finding and exploiting weaknesses; it was also seen in the Barracuda breach reported on 17 Nov.

Confirmed
Detected 22 Oct, confirmed 19 Nov. Payroll, leave and other staff systems were disrupted for more than 10 days. CERT-EU (the EU institutions' cyber team) identified ARTEX. No evidence of a broad staff data breach; attacker unknown.[5]
Why it matters
AI tools let attackers move at machine speed; detection must keep up.
MediumConfidence: MediumUnpatched flaw

FastUnlock: running programs on a locked Windows PC

What happened
A researcher published a way for someone holding a locked Windows PC to start programs using the built-in Narrator screen reader and recovery options. It gives a limited account, not admin. Security firm ThreatLocker confirmed it works; no fix yet and no attacks seen.[6]
Action
Add a startup PIN to BitLocker disk encryption and shut down laptops when they leave your control.

Java4Shell: attack attempts begin

Background: Java runs much of banks' business software. Java4Shell is a flaw in how Java decodes Base64 text that can let an attacker take over some Java services without a password on servers with certain recent Intel and AMD chips.

What's new
GreyNoise, watchTowr, Cloudflare and Previdian saw scanning and early attack attempts on 18–19 Nov; none confirmed successful. Cisco says more than a dozen products are affected with no fixes yet; Elastic, Red Hat, Jenkins and Keycloak are affected; Atlassian advises Data Center customers to switch off a chip feature (AVX-512) until patched. Amazon, Microsoft and Azul released fixed Java builds.[2]
Action
Patch Java everywhere, including copies bundled inside vendor products, and track vendor advisories.
HighConfidence: HighUpdate

nginx2root: five critical flaws, two public exploits

Background: NGINX is web server software (owned by F5) in front of a huge share of websites; the flaw is in its HTTP/2 handling.

What's new
F5's patches cover five critical NGINX2Root flaws plus 17 other NGINX and BIG-IP bugs. Rapid7 and Censys published test exploits. Still no attacks reported.[8]
HighConfidence: HighUpdate

PHP releases fixes for seven critical flaws

Background: PHP is the programming language behind many websites. Last month's related "PHP2Shell" flaws are widely exploited.

What's new
Released 19 Nov. Most could allow code execution but need specific application setups; one was an incomplete fix for PHP2Shell. None reported exploited.[7]

92-minute security fix

Clear out old apps that can read your email 2 minutes · phone or laptop · your personal accounts

Why now: this week's N-able breach hit a service that filters email for thousands of businesses. Every app you once allowed to "sign in with Google" or read your inbox is another way into your account, and most of us have forgotten half of them.

  1. Google: open myaccount.google.com/connections (Security → Your connections to third-party apps).
  2. Microsoft: open account.live.com/consent/Manage. Apple: Settings → your name → Sign in with Apple.
  3. Remove anything you don't recognise or haven't used for months, starting with anything that can see your email, files or contacts.

Done when: only apps you actually use are left. Bonus: do the same for a family member's account.

For your work account, leave this to your IT team.

Glossary

Third-party app access
Permission you give an outside app (often via "Sign in with Google/Microsoft") to read parts of your account, such as email or files; it lasts until you remove it.
Virtual machine
A software copy of a computer that runs alongside many others on one physical server.
Zero-day
A flaw attackers use before the maker has a fix.
Single sign-on (SSO)
One login that opens many systems; its signing keys prove logins are genuine.
Root
The all-powerful administrator account on Linux-type systems.
Proof of concept (PoC)
Test code showing a flaw can be exploited; criminals often adapt it.
Base64
A common way of turning data into plain text characters.
HTTP/2
A faster version of the protocol browsers and servers use to talk.
Ransomware
Malware that locks or steals data and demands payment.
LLMjacking
Stealing access to paid AI models to use them at the victim's expense.
Cryptomining
Secretly using a victim's computers to generate cryptocurrency.
BitLocker
Windows' built-in disk encryption.
API
A connection that lets programs talk to a service directly.
CISA exploited list (KEV)
The US cyber agency's list of flaws confirmed as used in real attacks.
