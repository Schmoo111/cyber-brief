2New incidents (past 24 hours)

HighConfidence: MediumPayment data breach

Cleverbridge: card numbers and security codes stolen

Background: Cleverbridge runs online shops and subscription billing for software companies; its listed clients include Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf and Sony. Card rules say the three-digit security code (CVV) should not be kept after a payment is approved, so finding it in stolen data is unusual.

Confirmed (Cleverbridge)
It spotted intruders on 4 Nov and briefly took systems offline. Its investigation found someone copied stored card numbers, expiry dates and CVVs; it says names and addresses were not taken and it was not ransomware. Notices went out from 20 Nov; regulators, police and card networks were told; free credit monitoring is offered.[1]
Unknown
How many cards, how attackers got in, why CVVs were stored, and who did it. No group has claimed it.
Why it matters
Number, expiry and CVV together are enough for online fraud. Card issuers, banks included, may need to reissue cards and tune fraud rules for these merchants.
HighConfidence: MediumSupply-chain / device malware

GL.iNet: cloud tool used to infect routers and KVM devices

Background: GL.iNet makes small home and travel routers and "KVM" boxes, which let someone control a computer remotely as if sitting at it. Its GoodCloud service manages those devices over the internet, so a weakness there reaches every connected device at once.

Confirmed (GL.iNet)
An unauthorised party exploited its cloud management service to install malware. GL.iNet says the hole is fixed, but this does not clean devices already infected; new firmware was due 22 Nov.[4]
Researchers found
One consultancy found suspicious activity on 152 of 183 routers it checked, some with hidden backdoors. The malware hides itself and survives restarts. Its code has Chinese-language comments; that is not an attribution.[4]
Action
Install the latest firmware, reinstall without restoring old settings if infection is suspected, and change any passwords the device held.
Why it matters
Staff use travel routers on the road; KVMs sit next to servers. An infected one is a foothold.
MediumConfidence: MediumCloud provider breach

Rackspace Spot: leaked logins exposed customers' secrets

Background: Rackspace is a US cloud hosting firm. Spot, built with partner Platform9, runs customers' software in "containers" managed by Kubernetes, a popular system for running many small apps; "Kubernetes Secrets" are where those apps keep their passwords and keys.

Confirmed (Rackspace)
On 17 Nov someone used leaked credentials to act as real users in some customer accounts, start their own software and read stored secrets. Found 18 Nov after a customer report; service fully restored 21 Nov.[5]
Action
Customers should reissue access tokens, change every password stored in their secrets and check logs back to 17 Nov.
LowConfidence: MediumBotnet / ad fraud

BADBOX 3.0: cheap TV boxes back in a record fraud network

Background: BADBOX is malware pre-installed on cheap, uncertified Android TV boxes, tablets and phones. Infected devices join a "botnet" used for advertising fraud and to route criminals' internet traffic through people's homes.

Researchers found
HUMAN, an anti-fraud firm, says the new version peaked at about 40 billion fake ad requests a day, a record. It links the malware to a Chinese electronics maker; Google has sued earlier operators.[6]
Why it matters
Fraudsters use such home connections to disguise logins to online banking.
LowConfidence: HighPrivacy

Signal blocks phone AI assistants from reading chats

Confirmed (Signal)
The encrypted messaging app now blocks screenshots by default, so built-in AI agents on iPhone and Android cannot read conversations off the screen. Signal admits the protection is incomplete.[7]
Why it matters
Phone AI assistants can see whatever is on screen, including work chats.

92-minute security fix

Lock the bank cards you rarely use 2 minutes · phone · your personal banking apps

Why now: the Cleverbridge breach shows card numbers and security codes can be stolen from a shop you used once, years ago. A locked card can't be charged, and you can unlock it in seconds when you need it.

  1. Open your bank or card provider's app and tap the card you seldom use (a backup credit card, an old store card).
  2. Look for Freeze card, Lock card or Card controls and switch it on. Most UK, US and EU banks offer this.
  3. While there, check for an option to block online or overseas payments on cards you only use in person.

Done when: every card you don't use weekly shows as frozen or locked. If your bank has no freeze option, call the number on the card and ask about a temporary block. Bonus: show a parent or teenager where the button is.

For your work devices and accounts, leave this to your IT team.

Glossary

CVV
The three- or four-digit security code on a payment card, used to prove you hold it when buying online.
Card-not-present fraud
Fraud using stolen card details online or by phone, without the physical card.
Card freeze / lock
A banking-app switch that temporarily blocks new payments on a card.
KVM device
A box that lets someone control a computer's keyboard, screen and mouse remotely.
Firmware
The built-in software that runs a device such as a router.
Backdoor
A hidden way back into a system that an attacker leaves behind.
Credentials
Login details such as usernames, passwords and access keys.
Container / Kubernetes
A container is a packaged app; Kubernetes is a popular system for running many of them.
Kubernetes Secrets
Where apps running on Kubernetes keep their passwords and keys.
Access token
A digital pass that lets software act as a user without retyping a password.
Botnet
A network of infected devices controlled together by criminals.
Residential proxy
A service that routes someone's internet traffic through a home connection to disguise where it comes from.
AI agent
An AI that carries out tasks on its own, such as reading the screen or sending messages.
Zero-day
A flaw attackers use before the maker has a fix.
Hard-coded password
A password built into a product that customers can't change.
CISA exploited list (KEV)
The US cyber agency's list of flaws confirmed as used in attacks, with fix deadlines for US agencies.
HTTP/2
A faster version of the protocol browsers and servers use to talk.
Ransomware
Malicious software that locks a victim's files until a ransom is paid.

Access token

A digital pass that lets software act as a user without retyping a password.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026

Accessibility services

Phone features that let apps read and control the screen for disabled users; malware abuses them.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Account takeover

A criminal gaining control of someone's online account, often by tricking them into handing over login details.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

AI agent

An AI that carries out tasks on its own, such as reading the screen or sending messages.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026

AI model

The trained "brain" file behind an AI tool. Swapping it changes what the tool does without changing the app around it.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026

API

A connection that lets programs talk to a service directly.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

ASLR

A protection that shuffles where programs sit in memory so attackers can't easily aim at them.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026

Backdoor

A hidden way back into a system that an attacker leaves behind.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026

Base64

A common way of turning data into plain text characters.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026, 20 Nov 2026

BitLocker

Windows' built-in disk encryption.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

Botnet

A network of infected devices controlled together by criminals.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Buffer overflow

Writing more data into a memory space than it can hold, which can crash a program or let an attacker run code.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026, 21 Nov 2026

Canary token

A fake password or link planted to reveal anyone who uses it.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Card freeze / lock

A banking-app switch that temporarily blocks new payments on a card.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Card skimming

Hidden code on a payment page that copies card details as customers type them.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026

Card-not-present fraud

Fraud using stolen card details online or by phone, without the physical card.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Certificate

A digital ID card that lets computers prove who they are to each other.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026

CISA exploited list (KEV)

The US cyber agency's list of flaws confirmed as used in attacks, with fix deadlines for US agencies.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026, 19 Nov 2026, 20 Nov 2026, 21 Nov 2026, 22 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026

Container / Kubernetes

A container is a packaged app; Kubernetes is a popular system for running many of them.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Credentials

Login details such as usernames, passwords and access keys.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Credit monitoring

A service that warns you if someone applies for credit in your name.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Cryptomining

Secretly using a victim's computers to generate cryptocurrency.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

CVE

A standard ID number given to each publicly known software flaw, e.g. CVE-2026-115731.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026

CVV

The three- or four-digit security code on a payment card, used to prove you hold it when buying online.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Data extortion

Stealing data and threatening to publish it unless paid.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

DDoS

Flooding a website with traffic so real users can't reach it.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Deserialisation flaw

A bug where a program unpacks data from outside without checking it, letting attackers slip in commands.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

DNS

The internet's address book, turning names into network addresses.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Double free / use-after-free

Memory bugs where a program releases memory twice, or keeps using it after releasing it; attackers can abuse both.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

End of life

A product the maker no longer fixes.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Entra ID

Microsoft's sign-in and identity system for businesses (formerly Azure Active Directory).

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Extortion group

Criminals who steal data and demand payment not to publish it.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026, 19 Nov 2026

Firmware

The built-in software that runs a device such as a router.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Hard-coded password

A password built into a product that customers can't change.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026

Hotfix

An urgent, narrowly tested fix released before a full software update.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026

HTTP/2

A faster version of the protocol browsers and servers use to talk.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026, 20 Nov 2026, 21 Nov 2026, 22 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026

Inference provider

A firm that runs AI models on its computers and answers customers' prompts.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

iRule

A custom traffic-handling rule written by administrators on F5 devices.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026

Java / OpenJDK

Java is a programming platform used to build and run a large share of business software. OpenJDK is its free, open-source core, which Oracle, Amazon, Red Hat and others package as their own versions.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026

JNDI

A Java feature that looks up resources by name; if an attacker controls the lookup, the server can be tricked into talking to them.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Kernel

The core of an operating system, which controls memory and hardware.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Kubernetes Secrets

Where apps running on Kubernetes keep their passwords and keys.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

KVM device

A box that lets someone control a computer's keyboard, screen and mouse remotely.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Library

A shared building block of code that many programs use.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

LLMjacking

Stealing access to paid AI models to use them at the victim's expense.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

OpenPGP key

A digital key used to encrypt or sign email.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Penetration testing

Authorised hacking to find weaknesses before criminals do.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Phishing

Fake messages designed to trick people into giving up passwords or clicking malicious links.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026

Phishing-resistant sign-in

Login methods, such as passkeys or security keys, that can't be passed to a fake website.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Prompt

The text or request someone sends to an AI model.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Proof of concept (PoC)

Test code showing a flaw can be exploited; criminals often adapt it.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

Puppet server / agents

In Puppet Enterprise, the central server that sends configuration instructions, and the helper programs on each managed server that receive them.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026

Push alert

A message an app pops up on your phone's screen straight away.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

PyPI

The Python Package Index, the main public store where developers download Python code libraries.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026

Ransomware

Malicious software that locks a victim's files until a ransom is paid.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026, 22 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026

Remote code execution

Running your own commands on someone else's computer over the network.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026, 19 Nov 2026, 21 Nov 2026

Residential proxy

A service that routes someone's internet traffic through a home connection to disguise where it comes from.

Used in: 23 Nov 2026

Root

The all-powerful administrator account on Linux-type systems.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

Sandbox

A walled-off area where risky files are handled so they can't touch the rest of the system.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Single sign-on (SSO)

One login that opens many systems; its signing keys prove logins are genuine.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

Supply-chain attack

Attacking a supplier's product so that the damage spreads to all of that supplier's customers.

Used in: 18 Nov 2026

Targeted phishing

Fake messages written for one named person, to trick them into handing over access.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Tenant

A company's own private space inside a cloud service such as Microsoft's.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Test environment

A copy of a system used to try changes; it should hold fake data.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Third-party (supplier) risk

The danger that a company you share data or access with is breached instead of you.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Third-party app access

Permission you give an outside app (often via "Sign in with Google/Microsoft") to read parts of your account, such as email or files; it lasts until you remove it.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

Virtual machine

A software copy of a computer that runs alongside many others on one physical server.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026

Virtual machine / nested virtualisation

A software copy of a computer; nested means running virtual machines inside a virtual machine.

Used in: 21 Nov 2026

Web server

Software that receives requests from browsers and apps and sends back web pages or data.

Used in: 19 Nov 2026

Zero data retention

A promise that prompts are not stored after they are answered.

Used in: 22 Nov 2026

Zero-day

A flaw attackers use before the maker has a fix.

Used in: 20 Nov 2026, 22 Nov 2026, 23 Nov 2026
